What we're proposing

Give each kind of patient their own way in: couples, female couples, single women, single men, trans and non-binary people, people planning ahead, NHS referrals and people moving from another clinic. Each shows the likely route, the first paid step and what to do next. Alongside it sit a four-question check of which package is likely to fit, a plain NHS funding check and, on a second page, the journey from first enquiry to the Rosie.

Why

The homepage already names every one of these groups in its opening paragraph, and then the menu is organised by service: assessments, treatments, preservation, donors. Female couples, single women and people freezing eggs are almost all self-funded, so they are the most valuable visitors the site gets, and they have to translate the clinic's structure into their own situation. Every competitor we looked at speaks to them by name. Meanwhile the NHS funding page still talks about CCGs, which were replaced in 2022, and the "Your journey" link in the menu goes to "Page not found".

Where would you like to start?

Whoever you are and whatever your situation, there's a route to treatment here. Tell us a little about you and we'll show you the likely steps, costs and the best first thing to do.

Which package is likely to fit?

Four questions. Your consultant confirms the right package once they've seen your results.

Your BMI: –

Leave blank if you haven't had the test. It's a blood test that estimates egg reserve (£121 with us).

Have you had IVF before, or a natural pregnancy?

Please answer all four questions. Age should be 18 to 60 and BMI a number like 24.5.

Why we ask

Our fixed-price packages are designed around age, egg reserve (AMH) and BMI, because they shape how much medication you need and how many eggs are likely. Answering now saves reading four package pages to find the one that applies to you.

Open questionThe rules here follow the prices page. The PURE page gives different ones (under 38, AMH above 10, BMI below 30). One set needs agreeing before this goes live.

Can I get NHS-funded treatment?

NHS funding for IVF is decided by the integrated care board (ICB) for the area where your GP is. We treat NHS-funded patients from the East of England, Hertfordshire and West Essex.

Not eligible? Self-funded IVF starts at £2,900.

Open questionEach ICB's current criteria need checking and linking. Today the live page says Cambridge and Peterborough CCG "have recently announced" that NHS IVF is returning, with details "to be added".

BehaviourPeople who find they aren't NHS-eligible are the clinic's next self-funded patients, so the answer always ends with the self-funded route.
