Prototype 5 of 5 · Self-pay services: egg freezing

What we're proposing

Turn the egg freezing page into a proper hub for people planning ahead: who it's for, what happens, the full cost including storage over the years, what affects the chance of success later, honest answers to the common questions, the next information session and a clear way to book a consultation. A second page brings the clinic's other self-pay services online: semen analysis booking, a donor pre-screen and storage payments.

Why

Egg freezing is paid for privately, researched for months and worth £3,675 to £7,875 up front, followed by £346 a year in storage for as long as the eggs are kept. The current page is about 750 characters long, with no price, no answers to common questions and a link to the prices page on the old domain. Bourn Hall and CARE both give it a section of its own. It's one of the few services where a better page should translate quite directly into more bookings, and the storage income lasts for years.

Fertility preservation

Egg freezing

Freezing your eggs gives you more options later. Here's who it's for, what happens, what it really costs over time and how to find out if it's right for you.

Who it's for

Planning ahead

You'd like children one day, but not yet.

Before medical treatment

Chemotherapy or surgery may affect your fertility. Often NHS-funded, and seen quickly: the current wait is one working day.

Before transition

Preserving eggs before gender-affirming treatment.

What happens

  1. Fertility assessment, including an AMH blood test and a scan
  2. Consultation to plan your cycle
  3. About two weeks of hormone injections at home, with monitoring scans
  4. Egg collection under sedation at Kefford House
  5. Eggs frozen and stored until you're ready

What it costs, including storage

For 5 years. Storage can be renewed for up to 55 years under UK law, with consent renewed every ten.

Total over that time

    Packages include medication, scans and blood tests, egg collection, freezing, the HFEA fee and the first year of storage.

    What affects the chance of success later

    The most important factor is your age when the eggs are frozen, followed by how many eggs are stored. Freezing eggs isn't a guarantee of a baby later, and we'll talk honestly about what's realistic for you.
