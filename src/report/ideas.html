A good clinic with a website that makes people work too hard to become patients.

Cambridge IVF has a 5 out of 5 HFEA inspection, published prices, staged payments, free information sessions and a story no private clinic can tell: it's NHS, it's part of CUH and its patients go on to the Rosie. The website buries most of that. There is no way to enquire online, the prices page is hard to read and contradicts itself, and the success rates are five years old. Patients notice: 16 of the 22 people who rated the clinic on the HFEA site say they paid "way above the estimate".

Below are what we found, how the nearest competitors do it, 43 ideas ranked by how directly they help the clinic earn from a visitor, five bigger concepts to prototype, and how the clinic could build most of it with the platform it already has.

What we found

From walking the site in a browser at desktop and phone sizes, reading its stylesheet and checking the HFEA's public profile of the clinic. Every point links to the page it's about.

Working well

  • Prices are published in full, with staged payments for Duo and Multi-Cycle packages. Many clinics hide this. Prices
  • Regulator's confidence: 5 out of 5 inspection rating, licensed to 2030, live birth rate "consistent with national average". HFEA profile
  • Inclusive from the first line: the hero names heterosexual couples, same sex couples, single women, single men and transgender people. Homepage
  • Free information sessions with an embryologist, a nurse and the finance manager. Information sessions
  • Live wait times for andrology, updated this week. Wait times
  • The NHS story: CUH genetics, counselling within two weeks, care through to the Rosie. Pregnancy care
  • Honest framing on success rates, quoting the HFEA that most clinics are "broadly all as good as each other". Success rates

Holding it back

  • No way to enquire online. No forms on any of the 50 or so pages checked; self-referral is "by email or post". Referrals
  • Cost surprises. 16 of 22 HFEA reviewers paid "way above the estimate", while the prices page promises no hidden extras. HFEA profile
  • Contradictory criteria. PURE: 37 and under, AMH 6–35, BMI below 35 on one page; under 38, AMH above 10, BMI below 30 on another. PURE page
  • Add-ons without the evidence. EmbryoScope and PICSI are rated "no effect" by the HFEA, endometrial receptivity testing is rated red, and none of this appears on the site. HFEA add-ons
  • A hard prices page. About 8,000px on a phone, 34 accordions, the add-on list repeated under nearly every package, and typos. Prices
  • Dated copy. CCGs (gone since 2022), a former health minister, a closed petition, 2019–2020 success data shown as images, stories from 2013. Sources of support
  • Broken menu and theme. Two menu links return "Page not found", and inner pages show NHS blue because of a broken theme setting. Your journey (404)
  • Service-led navigation. The most valuable self-funded visitors (female couples, single women, egg freezers) have no entry point of their own. Services

How the competition does it

Five clinics: the nearest private rival, the biggest national group, the private clinic in Cambridge IVF's Herts and West Essex catchment, and two NHS units that have modernised their self-funded offer. Only what we saw is recorded; "n/c" means not checked, not missing.

Bourn Hall Clinic

The nearest private rival: Bourn, about ten miles west of Cambridge, plus clinics in Colchester, Norwich, Wickford and Stamford. Same patients, same searches.

  • Menu by who you are: couples, female couples, male couples, single women, single men
  • 'Book a discovery call' as the main button, plus an embedded enquiry form
  • Fertility self-assessment and an instant fertility report
  • Open evenings, including one in Cambridge
  • Finance, multi-cycle savings and an IVF refund programme
  • Success rates by age, using 2025 outcomes
  • Doctify reviews, patient portal, online egg donor pre-screen

CARE Fertility

The largest UK group and the standard most self-funders compare against.

  • 'Where to start' by audience, including trans and non-binary people and over-40s
  • NHS eligibility calculator and BMI calculator
  • Personalised success prediction (Univfy)
  • Online events
  • Carepay finance, Trustpilot reviews, patient portal

Herts & Essex Fertility Centre

Cheshunt. Competes directly in Cambridge IVF's Hertfordshire and West Essex NHS catchment.

  • 'Are you…' menu listing eight patient situations
  • Next free open evening date on the homepage, with an events calendar
  • Book a consultation form
  • Cancellation and refund policy page, funding options
  • Meet our team, consent portal, online sperm bank

The Hewitt Fertility Centre

Liverpool. An NHS unit with a self-funded offer, so the closest model for what Cambridge IVF could be online.

  • 'How can we help you?' menu by situation
  • Profiles of every consultant and lead
  • Award-winning patient portal (HSJ 2024) and digital consent
  • Virtual appointments (Attend Anywhere)
  • Self-funded 'Hewitt Options' with a fee schedule and financial packages

Guy's and St Thomas' Specialist Care

London. An NHS trust selling self-funded fertility care with a modern, transparent prices page.

  • Eligibility criteria stated up front, on the prices page
  • "Only evidence-based treatments… no unnecessary add-ons"
  • Personalised costed treatment plan for every patient
  • Enquiry form on the prices page
  • Online payments through GOV.UK Pay; evening and weekend appointments

What they have in common

Every one of them lets you enquire or book without picking up the phone, and four of the five organise their menus around the patient rather than the service. The two NHS units show that an NHS clinic can do this too.

Five concepts to prototype

Each concept combines several ideas into one piece of work that can be shown as a clickable prototype in Cambridge IVF's own look. The quick wins sit alongside as small, standalone changes.

Concept 1

Clear costs

An honest total before the first appointment: estimator, side-by-side packages, add-ons with their HFEA ratings and ways to pay.

Tackles the clinic's worst public number (16 of 22 HFEA reviewers paid 'way above the estimate') and is the clearest route to more multi-cycle sales.

Ideas: 1 2 3 6 7 8 32

Pays back through: Fewer drop-outs after consultation, more Duo and Multi-Cycle packages, a better HFEA patient rating.
Concept 2

Find your path

Entry points for each kind of patient, a quick eligibility check, and the journey from enquiry to the Rosie on one page.

The site is organised the way the clinic works, by service. Patients arrive as a couple, a single woman, a female couple, someone planning ahead or someone not eligible for NHS funding.

Ideas: 4 15 16 28 41

Pays back through: Puts the most valuable self-funded visitors on the right price and next step in one or two clicks.
Concept 3

Start today

Enquire, book a call or an information session without leaving the page, at any hour, and measure every lead.

There is no form anywhere on the site. Every competitor checked captures enquiries on the page.

Ideas: 9 10 11 12 13 14

Pays back through: The biggest single lever: more enquiries, captured out of hours, followed up faster, and finally counted.
Concept 4

Proof you can trust

Current success rates by age, real people, the team, the NHS difference and a plan to lift the HFEA patient rating.

Five-year-old charts, 2013 stories and a 2 out of 5 HFEA patient rating undersell a clinic with a 5 out of 5 inspection.

Ideas: 21 23 24 25 26

Pays back through: Wins more of the people who shortlist on the HFEA site and compare Cambridge IVF with Bourn Hall.
Concept 5

Self-pay services hub

An egg freezing hub, online booking for private semen analysis, a donor pre-screen and online storage payments.

These are the services people pay for themselves, often without a referral, and the site currently treats them as footnotes.

Ideas: 36 37 38 39

Pays back through: New self-funded revenue (egg freezing from £3,675 plus £346 a year storage) and faster collection of recurring fees.

Doing it yourselves

The site runs on Wagtail, a Django content management system, on a platform shared with the rest of Cambridge University Hospitals (one stylesheet carries themes for CUH, the Rosie, Cambridge Children's and others). That shapes who can do what.

Editor, in Wagtail (25 ideas)

Copy fixes, menu links, new pages and sections built from the blocks the site already has (accordions, teasers, inset text, the pink call-to-action panel, document lists, video). Most quick wins are here and need no developer. Agree an owner and an hour a week.

CUH web team or supplier (8 ideas)

Template fixes (the broken IVF theme), new content blocks (comparison table, estimator, eligibility checker, sticky next-step panel) and a price "snippet" so one figure feeds every page. Raise these as one change request. Most blocks would be reusable by other CUH services, which makes them easier to justify.

Third-party tool (6 ideas)

Forms, booking and payments. Because enquiries contain health information, use a tool the Trust already approves and complete a data protection impact assessment first. TryBooking is already in use for sessions; GOV.UK Pay is what Guy's and St Thomas' uses for payments.

Process (4 ideas)

Things the clinic does rather than builds: asking patients to rate it on the HFEA site, collecting stories, agreeing a finance option, and a monthly report from the GA4 and Tag Manager setup that is already installed.

A sensible order

  1. This month: the editor quick wins, starting with the PURE criteria, the broken menu links, the NHS funding page and the add-on ratings. Turn on GA4 event tracking so there's a before-and-after.
  2. Next: start asking every patient for an HFEA rating, and put the next information session dates on the homepage.
  3. Then: an online enquiry form (concept 3), because it makes every later change measurable.
  4. After that: clear costs (concept 1) and find your path (concept 2), which need the CUH web team.
  5. Alongside: the egg freezing hub and success-rate refresh, which are mostly editorial.
