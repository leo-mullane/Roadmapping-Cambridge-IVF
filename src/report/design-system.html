Principles behind the look

Cambridge IVF sits on the Cambridge University Hospitals (CUH) web platform, which is built on the NHS.UK design system. The IVF brand is a theme laid over it: NHS dark pink instead of NHS blue for the big moments.

NHS first

Frutiger, square corners, NHS blue links and the yellow-aqua focus state. Patients should feel they are on an NHS site, because they are.

Pink for warmth

#7C2855 carries headings, the hero button, the footer rule and the big call-to-action panels. It is what separates Cambridge IVF from the rest of CUH.

Heavy headings, plain body

Headings are 800 weight and tight (line-height 1). Body copy is 16 to 18px, regular, generous line-height.

Flat and square

No radius, no shadows, 1px borders in #D8DDE0, grey #F0F4F5 bands to separate sections.

Colour and contrast

Core palette, with WCAG 2.2 contrast ratios measured against white and the grey surface. AA needs 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text.

Layout and grid

Container

1280px maximum, 20px side gutters, 1240px content width inside the large wrapper. Sections are spaced by 50px top and bottom.

Breakpoints

480, 641 (40.0625em, from NHS.UK), 768, 1024 and 1280px. The header collapses to a Menu button below 1024px.

Header

White, 153px tall at desktop including the utility bar. Nav items have 38px vertical padding and a 5px bottom rule on hover/active.

One thing to fix while you're in there

Inner pages carry the body class theme-- -ivf, which is a broken theme name. The IVF theme rules never apply, so every inner page falls back to an NHS blue hero (left) rather than the IVF treatment the theme defines (right). Content also sits in a 640px column with nothing on the right at desktop.

Voice and content

Warm, inclusive and NHS-plain. The best copy on the site speaks to "you" and names who the clinic is for. The weakest copy is promotional, dated or inconsistent.

Do

  • Name the people you treat: couples, same sex couples, single women, single men, transgender people.
  • Give the number: "£5,240, paid in one go" or "seen within two weeks".
  • Say what is included and what is not, in the same place as the price.
  • Point to the HFEA for comparison and ratings, as the success rates page already does.
  • Use plain words for treatment steps, with the clinical term after it.

Don't

  • Use superlatives you can't evidence ("the best success rates in Europe").
  • Leave dated news in evergreen pages (ministers, CCGs, petitions, 2013 openings).
  • State eligibility differently on two pages.
  • Use the NHS "Non-urgent advice" card for general information.
  • Invent quotes or testimonials. Prototypes use [bracketed placeholders] instead.
